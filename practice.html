Time, Speed & Distance
Appears 92% of years • 2-3 questions per paper • Difficulty: Moderate to Hard
1. Relative Speed (Two people/objects moving) 🔥 Very Likely 2026

When two objects move in same/opposite direction. Same direction: relative speed = difference. Opposite: relative speed = sum.

Practice Question 1 — Same Direction Chase
A starts from point P at 6 AM walking at 4 km/hr. B starts from the same point at 8 AM cycling at 12 km/hr in the same direction. At what time will B catch A?
Show Answer & Approach
Solution
By 8 AM, A has covered 4 × 2 = 8 km. Relative speed = 12 − 4 = 8 km/hr. Time to catch up = 8/8 = 1 hour. B catches A at 9 AM, at a distance of 12 km from P.
Practice Question 2 — Opposite Direction Meeting
Two trains start from stations A and B, 300 km apart, at the same time towards each other. Train from A travels at 60 km/hr and train from B at 90 km/hr. At what distance from A will they meet?
Show Answer
Solution
Combined speed = 60 + 90 = 150 km/hr. Time to meet = 300/150 = 2 hours. Distance from A = 60 × 2 = 120 km. Ratio of distances = ratio of speeds = 60:90 = 2:3.
2. Circular Races / Circular Track 🔥 HIGH CHANCE 2026

People running on a circular track. First meeting = LCM concept. Same direction: time = circumference ÷ |speed difference|. Opposite: time = circumference ÷ (sum of speeds).

Practice Question 1 — Two Runners, Same Direction
A and B start from the same point on a circular track of length 600 m. A runs at 5 m/s and B runs at 3 m/s in the same direction. After how many seconds will they meet for the first time at the starting point?
Show Answer
Solution
Time for A to complete one round = 600/5 = 120s. Time for B = 600/3 = 200s. They meet at starting point = LCM(120, 200) = 600 seconds = 10 minutes.

First meeting anywhere on track = 600/(5−3) = 600/2 = 300 seconds.
Practice Question 2 — Three Runners
Three runners A, B, and C start together from the same point on a circular track of 1200 m. Their speeds are 6 m/s, 4 m/s, and 3 m/s respectively, all running in the same direction. When will all three meet for the first time?
Show Answer
Solution
A-B meet every 1200/(6−4) = 600s. A-C meet every 1200/(6−3) = 400s. All three meet = LCM(600, 400) = 1200 seconds = 20 minutes.
Practice Question 3 — Opposite Directions
A and B run on a circular track of 400 m in opposite directions at 8 m/s and 6 m/s. How many times do they meet in 10 minutes?
Show Answer
Solution
First meeting time = 400/(8+6) = 400/14 ≈ 28.57s. In 600s, meetings = floor(600/28.57) = 21 times.
🔮 2026 Prediction
Expect a 3-runner circular race problem where you need to find when all three meet at the starting point, with one runner going in the opposite direction.
3. Trains (Crossing poles, platforms, other trains) ⚡ Likely
Practice Question 1 — Train crosses platform
A train 200 m long crosses a platform 300 m long in 25 seconds. What is the speed of the train in km/hr?
Show Answer
Solution
Total distance = 200 + 300 = 500 m. Speed = 500/25 = 20 m/s = 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/hr.
Practice Question 2 — Two trains crossing
Two trains of lengths 150 m and 250 m are running in opposite directions at 40 km/hr and 50 km/hr. How long will they take to completely cross each other?
Show Answer
Solution
Total distance = 150 + 250 = 400 m. Relative speed = 40 + 50 = 90 km/hr = 25 m/s. Time = 400/25 = 16 seconds.
4. Boats & Streams ⚡ Likely

Downstream = boat + stream. Upstream = boat − stream.

Practice Question
A boat takes 6 hours to go from A to B downstream and 10 hours to return. If the speed of the stream is 3 km/hr, find the distance between A and B.
Show Answer
Solution
Let boat speed = b. Downstream: d = (b+3)×6. Upstream: d = (b−3)×10. So 6(b+3) = 10(b−3) → 6b+18 = 10b−30 → 4b = 48 → b = 12. Distance = (12+3)×6 = 90 km.
5. Average Speed (Different speeds for different legs) 🔥 Very Likely
Practice Question — Classic CAT Type
A person travels from A to B at 40 km/hr and returns at 60 km/hr. What is the average speed for the entire journey?
Show Answer
Solution
For same distance at different speeds: Average speed = 2ab/(a+b) = 2×40×60/(40+60) = 4800/100 = 48 km/hr. (NOT 50 — harmonic mean, not arithmetic mean!)
Practice Question — Three Speeds (Advanced)
A man travels equal distances at 3 km/hr, 4 km/hr, and 5 km/hr. Find his average speed for the entire journey.
Show Answer
Solution
Average speed = 3/(1/3 + 1/4 + 1/5) = 3/((20+15+12)/60) = 3/(47/60) = 180/47 ≈ 3.83 km/hr.
6. Races (Head start, dead heat) ⚡ Likely
Practice Question
In a 1000 m race, A beats B by 100 m and B beats C by 150 m. By how much does A beat C in the same race?
Show Answer
Solution
When A finishes 1000, B is at 900. When B finishes 1000, C is at 850 → ratio B:C = 1000:850. When B is at 900, C is at 900 × 850/1000 = 765. A beats C by 1000 − 765 = 235 m.
7. Clocks (Angle between hands, meetings) 📌 Possible
Practice Question
At what time between 4 and 5 o'clock will the minute and hour hands be at right angles?
Show Answer
Solution
At 4:00, angle = 120°. Minute hand gains 5.5° per minute over hour hand. For 90°: 120 − 5.5t = 90 → t = 30/5.5 = 60/11 ≈ 5 5/11 minutes past 4. Second time: 120 − 5.5t = −90 → t = 210/5.5 = 420/11 ≈ 38 2/11 minutes past 4.

Profit & Loss
Appears 96% of years • 2-3 questions per paper • Difficulty: Easy to Moderate
1. Successive Discounts🔥 Very Likely
Practice
A shopkeeper marks up the price of an item by 50% and then offers two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. What is the net profit or loss percentage?
Show Answer
Solution
Let CP = 100. MP = 150. After 20% discount = 150 × 0.8 = 120. After 10% discount = 120 × 0.9 = 108. Profit = 8%. Net profit = 8%.
2. Dishonest Dealer / Faulty Weights🔥 HIGH CHANCE 2026
Practice
A shopkeeper claims to sell at cost price but uses a weight of 900 gm instead of 1 kg. What is his profit percentage?
Show Answer
Solution
Profit% = (Error/True value − Error) × 100 = (100/900) × 100 = 11.11%.
Practice — Combined Cheating
A dealer cheats while buying (uses 1100 gm weight) and while selling (uses 900 gm weight). If he claims to sell at cost price, find his profit %.
Show Answer
Solution
He buys 1100 gm for price of 1000 gm and sells 900 gm for price of 1000 gm. Profit% = (1100/900 − 1) × 100 = (200/900) × 100 = 22.22%.
🔮 2026 Prediction
A dishonest dealer problem combined with markup/discount is very likely. "Marks up by x%, gives y% discount, and uses z gm weight — find actual profit%."
3. Partnership (Profit Sharing)⚡ Likely
Practice
A starts a business with ₹40,000. After 3 months, B joins with ₹60,000. At the end of the year, total profit is ₹28,000. Find B's share.
Show Answer
Solution
A's investment ratio = 40,000 × 12 = 4,80,000. B's = 60,000 × 9 = 5,40,000. Ratio = 48:54 = 8:9. B's share = (9/17) × 28,000 = ₹14,824.
4. CP-SP with Tax / Multiple Items⚡ Likely
Practice — CAT Style
A man buys 5 oranges for ₹3 and sells 3 oranges for ₹5. What is his profit percentage?
Show Answer
Solution
CP of 1 orange = 3/5 = ₹0.60. SP of 1 orange = 5/3 = ₹1.67. Profit% = (1.67 − 0.60)/0.60 × 100 = 177.78%. Shortcut: Profit% = (5×5 − 3×3)/(3×3) × 100 = (25−9)/9 × 100 = 177.78%.
5. Break-Even / No Profit No Loss📌 Possible
Practice
A trader buys 80 kg of rice at ₹40/kg. He sells 60 kg at ₹50/kg. At what price should he sell the remaining 20 kg to make a 25% overall profit?
Show Answer
Solution
Total CP = 80 × 40 = ₹3200. Target SP = 3200 × 1.25 = ₹4000. From 60 kg = 60 × 50 = ₹3000. Remaining needed = 4000 − 3000 = ₹1000 from 20 kg. Price = 1000/20 = ₹50/kg.

Time & Work
Appears 88% of years • 1-2 questions per paper • Difficulty: Moderate
1. Basic Efficiency — Two/Three Workers🔥 Very Likely
Practice
A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B can do it in 18 days. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves. How many more days will B take to finish the remaining work?
Show Answer
Solution
LCM(12,18) = 36 units. A's rate = 3 units/day, B's rate = 2 units/day. Together in 4 days = (3+2)×4 = 20 units. Remaining = 36−20 = 16 units. B alone = 16/2 = 8 days.
2. Pipes & Cisterns (with Leak)🔥 HIGH CHANCE 2026
Practice
Pipe A fills a tank in 12 hours, Pipe B fills it in 15 hours, and Pipe C empties it in 20 hours. If all three are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be full?
Show Answer
Solution
LCM(12,15,20) = 60 litres. A fills 5 l/hr, B fills 4 l/hr, C empties 3 l/hr. Net = 5+4−3 = 6 l/hr. Time = 60/6 = 10 hours.
Practice — Tank with Unknown Leak
A pipe fills a tank in 8 hours. Due to a leak, it takes 10 hours. If the tank is full, how long will the leak take to empty it?
Show Answer
Solution
Pipe rate = 1/8. Net rate = 1/10. Leak rate = 1/8 − 1/10 = (5−4)/40 = 1/40. Leak empties tank in 40 hours.
🔮 2026 Prediction
Pipes & Cisterns with alternating opening pattern (Pipe A open for 1 hr, then B for 1 hr, then C closes — cycle repeats) is a hot CAT question type.
3. Alternate Days / Cyclic Work⚡ Likely
Practice
A can do a work in 10 days, B in 12 days. They work on alternate days, starting with A. In how many days will the work be completed?
Show Answer
Solution
LCM(10,12) = 60 units. A = 6 units/day, B = 5 units/day. Per 2-day cycle = 6+5 = 11 units. After 10 days (5 cycles) = 55 units done. Day 11: A works, does 6 units but only 5 needed. A finishes in 5/6 day. Total = 10 + 5/6 = 10⅚ days.
4. Wages Based on Work📌 Possible
Practice
A and B together complete a job in 12 days for a total wage of ₹3600. A alone can do it in 20 days. Find A's wage.
Show Answer
Solution
A's rate = 1/20. Combined rate = 1/12. B's rate = 1/12 − 1/20 = 2/60 = 1/30. Work ratio A:B = (1/20):(1/30) = 3:2. A's wage = (3/5) × 3600 = ₹2160.

Number System
Appears 92% of years • 2-4 questions • Difficulty: Hard (Conceptual)
1. Remainders (Fermat, Wilson, Pattern)🔥 HIGH CHANCE 2026
Practice — Power Remainder
What is the remainder when 2^256 is divided by 17?
Show Answer
Solution
By Fermat's Little Theorem: 2^16 ≡ 1 (mod 17). 256 = 16 × 16. So 2^256 = (2^16)^16 ≡ 1^16 ≡ 1 (mod 17).
Practice — Successive Division
What is the remainder when 7^7^7 is divided by 5?
Show Answer
Solution
7 mod 5 = 2. So find 2^(7^7) mod 5. Cycle of 2's powers mod 5: 2,4,3,1 (period 4). Need 7^7 mod 4 = 3^7 mod 4 = 3 (since 3 mod 4 cycles as 3,1,3,1...). So 2^3 mod 5 = 8 mod 5 = 3.
2. Number of Factors & Divisors🔥 Very Likely
Practice
How many factors of 2^4 × 3^3 × 5^2 are multiples of 12?
Show Answer
Solution
12 = 2² × 3¹. Factor must have at least 2² and 3¹. Choices: power of 2: {2,3,4} = 3 choices. Power of 3: {1,2,3} = 3 choices. Power of 5: {0,1,2} = 3 choices. Total = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.
Practice — Odd Factors
How many odd factors does 360 have?
Show Answer
Solution
360 = 2³ × 3² × 5¹. For odd factors, power of 2 must be 0. Odd factors = (2+1)(1+1) = 6. They are: 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45.
3. Last Digit / Last Two Digits⚡ Likely
Practice
What is the unit digit of 7^2024?
Show Answer
Solution
Cycle of 7: 7, 9, 3, 1 (period 4). 2024 mod 4 = 0. When remainder is 0, take the last in cycle. Unit digit = 1.
4. HCF & LCM Applications⚡ Likely
Practice — Classic CAT Type
The LCM of two numbers is 1260 and their HCF is 12. If one number is 84, find the other.
Show Answer
Solution
LCM × HCF = Product of numbers. 1260 × 12 = 84 × x. x = 15120/84 = 180.
5. Highest Power in n!⚡ Likely
Practice
What is the highest power of 12 that divides 50! ?
Show Answer
Solution
12 = 2² × 3. Power of 2 in 50! = 25+12+6+3+1 = 47. Power of 3 in 50! = 16+5+1 = 22. For 12 = 2²×3: powers available = floor(47/2) = 23 for 2², and 22 for 3. Answer = min(23, 22) = 22.
6. Base System & Digital Sum📌 Possible
Practice
Convert (234)₅ to base 10.
Show Answer
Solution
2×5² + 3×5¹ + 4×5⁰ = 50 + 15 + 4 = 69.

Algebra
Appears 88% of years • 4-6 questions • Difficulty: Moderate to Hard
1. Linear Equations — Integer/Natural Number Solutions🔥 Very Likely
Practice
How many positive integer solutions exist for 3x + 5y = 100?
Show Answer
Solution
y = (100 − 3x)/5. For y to be positive integer: 100 − 3x must be divisible by 5 and positive. 3x ≡ 0 (mod 5), so x must be multiple of 5. x = 5,10,15,20,25,30. Check y > 0: x=30 → y=2 ✓, x=35 → y negative ✗. Solutions: x = 5,10,15,20,25,30 → 6 solutions.
🔮 2026 Prediction
"Number of non-negative integer solutions of ax + by + cz = n" type is a CAT favourite. Master the stars-and-bars + constraint approach.
2. Quadratic Equations — Max/Min Problems🔥 Very Likely
Practice
If x + y = 10, what is the maximum value of xy?
Show Answer
Solution
By AM-GM: xy is maximum when x = y = 5. Max xy = 25. Alternatively: xy = x(10−x) = −x² + 10x. Max at x = −b/2a = 10/2 = 5. Max value = 25.
Practice — CAT Style
What is the minimum value of x² + y² + z² if x + y + z = 12 and x, y, z are positive reals?
Show Answer
Solution
By Cauchy-Schwarz or by symmetry: minimum when x = y = z = 4. Min = 16 + 16 + 16 = 48.
3. Inequalities (Modulus-based)🔥 Rising Trend
Practice
Find the number of integer solutions of |x − 3| + |x + 5| < 12.
Show Answer
Solution
Critical points: x = 3, x = −5. Distance between them = 8 < 12, so solutions exist. For x > 3: (x−3)+(x+5) < 12 → 2x+2 < 12 → x < 5. For x < −5: (3−x)+(−x−5) < 12 → −2x−2 < 12 → x > −7. For −5 ≤ x ≤ 3: 8 < 12 (always true). Combined: −7 < x < 5. Integer solutions: −6,−5,...,4 → 11 solutions.
4. Functions (Composite, Piecewise)🔥 Rising Trend 2026
Practice
If f(x) = x² − 3x + 2, find the number of solutions of f(f(x)) = 0.
Show Answer
Solution
f(x) = (x−1)(x−2). f(f(x)) = 0 means f(x) = 1 or f(x) = 2. f(x) = 1: x²−3x+1 = 0 → 2 solutions. f(x) = 2: x²−3x = 0 → x(x−3)=0 → 2 solutions. Total = 4 solutions.
5. Logarithms⚡ Likely
Practice
If log₂(log₃(log₄(x))) = 0, find x.
Show Answer
Solution
log₂(log₃(log₄(x))) = 0 → log₃(log₄(x)) = 2⁰ = 1 → log₄(x) = 3¹ = 3 → x = 4³ = 64.
6. Sequences & Series (AP, GP, Special)⚡ Likely
Practice
The sum of an infinite GP is 12 and the sum of the squares of its terms is 48. Find the first term and common ratio.
Show Answer
Solution
S = a/(1−r) = 12. Sum of squares: a²/(1−r²) = 48. Dividing: a(1+r)/a = 48/12 → a/(1−r) × (1−r)/(1+r) × a = ... Simpler: a²/(1−r²) = a²/((1−r)(1+r)) = 48. From first: a = 12(1−r). So 144(1−r)²/((1−r)(1+r)) = 48 → 144(1−r)/(1+r) = 48 → 3(1−r) = 1+r → 3−3r = 1+r → r = 1/2, a = 6.

Geometry & Mensuration
Appears 84% of years • 2-3 questions • Difficulty: Hard • RISING TREND ↑
1. Triangles — Similar Triangles / Area Ratios🔥 Very Likely
Practice
In triangle ABC, D and E are points on AB and AC such that DE ∥ BC. If AD/DB = 2/3, find the ratio of areas of △ADE to △ABC.
Show Answer
Solution
AD/AB = 2/5. For similar triangles, area ratio = (side ratio)². Area(ADE)/Area(ABC) = (2/5)² = 4/25.
2. Circles — Tangent, Secant, Common Tangents🔥 Very Likely
Practice
Two circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm have their centres 10 cm apart. What is the length of their direct common tangent?
Show Answer
Solution
DCT = √(d² − (r₁−r₂)²) = √(100 − 4) = √96 = 4√6 cm.
3. Mensuration — 3D Solids (Rising!)🔥 Rising Trend 2026
Practice
A cone of height 12 cm and base radius 9 cm is filled with water. A sphere of maximum possible radius is placed in it. What fraction of the cone's volume is occupied by the sphere?
Show Answer
Solution
Slant height = √(144+81) = √225 = 15. Inradius of cone (sphere that fits) = Volume/Lateral SA × 3... Using formula: r = 3V/(πRl + πR²) simplified. For inscribed sphere in cone: r = Rh/(l+R) = 9×12/(15+9) = 108/24 = 4.5 cm. V_sphere = (4/3)π(4.5)³ = 121.5π. V_cone = (1/3)π(81)(12) = 324π. Fraction = 121.5/324 = 3/8 = 37.5%.
🔮 2026 Prediction
A problem involving a sphere inscribed in a cone/cylinder, or water displacement when a solid is submerged, is very likely. 3D mensuration has appeared in 4 of the last 5 CATs.
4. Coordinate Geometry⚡ Likely
Practice
Find the area of the triangle formed by the lines y = 0, x + y = 6, and x = 0.
Show Answer
Solution
Vertices: (0,0), (6,0), (0,6). Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × 6 × 6 = 18 sq units.

P&C, Probability, Set Theory
Appears 76% of years • 1-2 questions • Difficulty: Hard • High risk-reward
1. Arrangements with Constraints⚡ Likely
Practice
In how many ways can 5 boys and 3 girls be seated in a row such that no two girls sit together?
Show Answer
Solution
Arrange 5 boys: 5! = 120. Gaps for girls: _B_B_B_B_B_ = 6 gaps. Choose 3 from 6 and arrange: P(6,3) = 120. Total = 120 × 120 = 14,400.
2. Distribution / Identical & Distinct Objects⚡ Likely
Practice
In how many ways can 10 identical chocolates be distributed among 3 children such that each child gets at least 2?
Show Answer
Solution
Give 2 to each child first. Remaining = 10 − 6 = 4. Distribute 4 among 3 (each ≥ 0) = C(4+3−1, 3−1) = C(6,2) = 15.
3. Probability — Conditional & Bayes📌 Possible
Practice
A bag has 4 red and 6 blue balls. Two balls are drawn without replacement. What is the probability that both are red?
Show Answer
Solution
P = (4/10) × (3/9) = 12/90 = 2/15.
4. Set Theory — Venn Diagram Max/Min🔥 Very Likely 2026
Practice — CAT Favourite
In a class of 120 students: 80 like Cricket, 70 like Football, 60 like Hockey. 40 like both Cricket and Football, 30 like both Football and Hockey, 35 like both Cricket and Hockey, and 20 like all three. How many like none?
Show Answer
Solution
|C∪F∪H| = 80+70+60−40−30−35+20 = 125. But total = 120. So at least 5 students were counted extra → 0 like none is not possible with these numbers (inconsistent), OR the question should give total ≥ 125. If total = 150, then none = 150 − 125 = 25. Key insight: always check if n(A∪B∪C) ≤ Total.
🔮 2026 Prediction
"What is the maximum/minimum number of students who like exactly 2 of 3 subjects?" — This EXACT formulation is a CAT favourite. Practice max-min Venn problems extensively.

Ratios, Percentages, Averages, CI/SI, Mixtures
Appears 96% of years • 3-5 questions combined • Difficulty: Easy to Moderate
1. Successive Percentage Change🔥 Almost Certain
Practice
The price of a commodity increases by 20% and then decreases by 20%. What is the net percentage change?
Show Answer
Solution
Net effect = a + b + ab/100 = 20 + (−20) + (20)(−20)/100 = 0 − 4 = −4% (decrease). Remember: successive same% increase then decrease always gives a loss of (x/10)²%.
2. Mixtures & Alligation (Replacement)🔥 HIGH CHANCE 2026
Practice — Replacement Type
A vessel contains 60 litres of milk. 12 litres is taken out and replaced with water. This process is repeated 3 times. How much milk remains?
Show Answer
Solution
Milk remaining = 60 × (1 − 12/60)³ = 60 × (4/5)³ = 60 × 64/125 = 30.72 litres.
Practice — Alligation
In what ratio must tea at ₹60/kg be mixed with tea at ₹90/kg to get a mixture worth ₹70/kg?
Show Answer
Solution
By alligation: (90−70):(70−60) = 20:10 = 2:1.
3. CI vs SI / Compound Interest⚡ Likely
Practice
The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 10% per annum is ₹50. Find the principal.
Show Answer
Solution
Diff for 2 years = P × (r/100)² = P × (0.1)² = 0.01P = 50. P = ₹5,000.
4. Weighted Average / Group Problems⚡ Likely
Practice
Average of 30 students is 40. If the top 5 scorers are removed, the average drops to 35. What was the average of the top 5?
Show Answer
Solution
Total = 30 × 40 = 1200. Remaining 25 students total = 25 × 35 = 875. Top 5 total = 1200 − 875 = 325. Average = 325/5 = 65.

VARC — Question Types & Patterns
24 questions • 40 minutes • RC is 65-70% of this section
1. RC — Inference Questions (THE most common type)🔥 100% Certain

"Which of the following can be inferred from the passage?" — Tests whether you can draw logical conclusions that aren't explicitly stated but are supported by the text.

💡 Aditi's Tip: The answer is NEVER directly stated in the passage. It must be a logical extension. Eliminate options that are too extreme, too broad, or make assumptions beyond the passage.
🔮 Expected in 2026
6-8 inference questions across 4-5 passages. Passages on AI ethics, climate economics, and social psychology are trending.
2. RC — Main Idea / Primary Purpose🔥 Certain

"The primary purpose of the passage is to..." — Tests big-picture comprehension.

💡 Tip: Read the first and last paragraphs carefully. The main idea connects ALL paragraphs, not just one. Eliminate options that are too narrow (covers only one para) or too broad (goes beyond what's discussed).
3. RC — Author's Tone / Attitude🔥 Very Likely

"The author's attitude towards X can best be described as..." — Look for adjective choices carefully.

💡 Common tones in CAT: Critical but balanced, Cautiously optimistic, Skeptical, Analytical. It's almost NEVER "indifferent" or "hostile" — CAT passages are from academic sources.
4. RC — Strengthen / Weaken the Argument⚡ Likely

"Which of the following, if true, would most weaken the author's argument?"

💡 To weaken: Find the assumption behind the argument and attack it. To strengthen: Find evidence that supports the assumption.
5. Para Jumbles (TITA — No negative marking!)🔥 Certain — 2-3 questions

Rearrange 4-5 sentences into a coherent paragraph. Usually TITA format.

💡 Strategy: (1) Find the OPENING sentence — introduces a topic, no pronoun references. (2) Find MANDATORY PAIRS — sentences that must be adjacent due to pronoun reference or cause-effect. (3) Find the CLOSING sentence — conclusion or summary. (4) Build the sequence from these anchors.
🔮 2026 Prediction
Expect 2-3 Para Jumbles, all TITA. At least one will have 5 sentences (harder). Look for transition words: "However", "Moreover", "Consequently".
6. Para Summary🔥 Certain — 2-3 questions

Pick the best summary of a given paragraph from 4 options.

💡 Eliminate: Options that add info NOT in the passage, miss the MAIN point, are too specific, or distort the tone. The best summary covers the KEY argument without extra details.
7. Odd Sentence Out (TITA)🔥 Certain — 2-3 questions

5 sentences given, 4 form a coherent paragraph. Find the odd one out.

💡 Strategy: (1) Identify the THEME — what are 4 sentences about? (2) The odd one might be related but talks about a different ASPECT. (3) Often the odd sentence introduces a new entity or contradicts the flow. (4) Try forming the paragraph with each sentence removed — which removal makes the best paragraph?

DILR — Set Types & Predicted Patterns
20 questions in 4-5 sets • 40 minutes • Most unpredictable section
⚠️ Golden Rule for DILR, Aditi: Spend first 5 minutes SCANNING all sets. Pick 3-4 easy ones. Skip the hardest set entirely. Set SELECTION is more important than solving speed here.
1. Tables — Data Analysis🔥 Almost Certain

Partially filled table, derive missing values using given conditions. Often involves percentages, ratios, or growth rates.

🔮 2026 Prediction
A table with 5-6 rows and 4-5 columns with some cells blank. You'll need to figure out values from row/column totals and constraints. Expect 4 questions per set.
2. Arrangements (Linear & Circular)🔥 Very Likely

8-10 people seated in a row or around a table with multiple constraints (A sits 3rd from left, B not adjacent to C, etc.)

💡 Approach: Start with the MOST constrained person. Fill in definite positions first. Use elimination. Draw it out on paper!
🔮 2026 Prediction
A double-row arrangement (people sitting across a table facing each other) with 3-4 attributes per person is very likely. This type appeared in CAT 2022 and 2024.
3. Games & Tournaments🔥 CAT FAVOURITE — Very Likely

Round-robin or knockout tournaments. You'll need to figure out wins, losses, draws, points from partial information.

🔮 2026 Prediction
A tournament with 6-8 teams, league format (every team plays every other). Given: total points, some match results, constraints like "Team A scored more than Team B". You figure out the full results table. This appeared in CAT 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024 — almost guaranteed.
4. Scheduling / Sequencing🔥 Rising — Very Likely

Tasks/events scheduled across time slots with constraints on ordering, duration, prerequisites.

🔮 2026 Prediction
"5 courses across 6 time slots, each student takes 3, constraints on which can't overlap" — this scheduling type is trending heavily.
5. Routes & Networks🆕 New Trend

Find shortest path, minimum cost route, or optimal delivery schedule on a network/map.

🔮 2026 Prediction
A delivery/logistics optimization set with 5-6 cities and routes between them. This is a newer CAT favourite aligned with real-world MBA problems.
6. Binary Logic / True-False⚡ Likely

Given statements from 4-5 people where each makes claims. Some always tell truth, some always lie. Determine who's truthful.

💡 Approach: Assume person A tells truth → check for consistency. If contradiction → A lies. Systematically test each assumption.
7. Multi-variable Puzzles (Grids)⚡ Likely

5 people, each with 3-4 attributes (city, profession, car colour, etc.). Determine the complete assignment using clues.

💡 Use a matrix/grid to mark possibilities. Start with clues that give definite information. Cross-reference to eliminate.
